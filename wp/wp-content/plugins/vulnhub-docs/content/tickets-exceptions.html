Two workflows turn findings into decisions: tickets (do the work) and exceptions (formally accept the risk). Both attach to findings and both move the numbers.

Tickets — driving remediation

A ticket is a remediation task, tracked in Jira, linked to one or more findings. You can raise one from a finding (or a group of findings), and VulnHub keeps it in step with Jira.

How tickets flow

RaiseFrom a finding, VulnHub creates a Jira issue (routed to the owning team's project) and links the findings to it. The finding then shows its ticket.
SyncTicket status is pulled back from Jira. The Ticket flow widget shows the split by status category — To do / In progress / Done.
ReopenIf a still-detected issue was closed, the reopener flags it — a closed ticket is a claim, not proof.
VerifyWhen a ticket closes, VulnHub waits a settling period then re-runs a narrowed scan to prove the finding is gone.

Verification — "closed" vs "actually fixed"

This is the point of the Closure verification widget. After a ticket is marked done, the finding's verification state moves through:

PendingClosed in Jira; not yet re-checked.
Confirmed fixedA rescan confirms the finding is gone. The good outcome.
Still detectedTenable still sees it — a false closure. The finding is flipped back to reopened.
Cannot verifyThe rescan was inconclusive.

The dashboard's "Closed but still detected" headline tile counts the still detected tickets — the closures you cannot trust.

SLAs

Each team has per-severity SLA targets (by default: Critical 7 days, High 30, Medium 90, Low 180). A finding's due date is set from its team's SLA, and the "Past SLA" / "Findings within SLA" figures are computed against it.

Automations

Ticket creation can be automated: rules match on finding attributes and raise (grouped) tickets on events, with throttles and per-run caps, so a flood of new findings doesn't create a flood of tickets. Automations are configured in the admin area.

Exceptions — accepting a risk on purpose

Sometimes the right answer is not "fix it" but "accept it, with a reason, for now". An exception records that decision and removes the affected findings from the live counts — visibly, with an audit trail, never silently.

Raising one

From a finding, request an exception. You choose:

ScopeThis finding (one finding on one asset), this asset (all its findings), this vulnerability (across all assets), or a team (one vulnerability across a team).
ReasonFalse positive, compensating control, no fix available, business impact, end of life, not exploitable, accepted risk, or other.
JustificationRequired, and at least 20 characters — the record an auditor will read.
ExpiryMandatory, and in the future. Exceptions are temporary, reviewed decisions.

The lifecycle of an exception

An exception moves draft → pending → approved (or rejected). Approval requires the approver capability. On approval it stamps the matching open findings, which immediately drop out of the live counts (a separate "excepted" figure keeps them visible). Each night, approved exceptions past their expiry flip to expired and their findings return to the counts — which is what the Exceptions expiring widget is warning you about. Revoking one clears its findings straight away. Every exception carries a reference (EXC-0001, …) so decisions are citable.

Why this matters for the numbers. An excepted finding is not deleted and not ignored — it is accounted for. That is the difference between a dashboard you can defend in an audit and one you can't. See Finding lifecycle & scoping for exactly how exceptions interact with counts.
