The Assets screen is the register of every machine VulnHub knows about — merged from all your systems into one row each — with its owner, what has and hasn't scanned it, which systems know it, and the open exposure it carries. It is where coverage-gap drill-downs land.

What one asset row represents

A single machine may be discovered by Tenable, managed by Intune, recorded in the CMDB and seen by Defender. VulnHub merges them by matching on the strongest identifier available (Tenable UUID → Intune id → Azure AD id → serial → hostname) and remembers every contributing system. So one row can carry facts from four sources, and the Known by column tells you which (the asset page also names the system that first discovered it).

Key fields

Hostname / IP / OSIdentity and platform. The OS release is what the End-of-life widgets match against (by build number, not label).
TypeWorkstation, server, mobile, network, cloud, appliance or unknown — set by the classification rules or a connector.
Criticality & environmentHow much the machine matters, and where it sits (production/test/…). Criticality multiplies the risk score of its findings.
Owner / team / locationWho to chase. Resolved by ownership mapping — from the Intune primary user (high confidence), or the CMDB custodian (medium), or a fallback (low).
Lifecycle statusIn service, quarantine, maintenance, spare, stock, planned, retired, missing or unknown — drives whether the asset is "reportable" and whether its findings are counted or archived.
Open critical / highPre-computed roll-ups of the asset's live findings by severity, and its overall risk score.

Coverage — the blind-spot view

Every asset carries a Tenable coverage state (the Scan coverage column) and a Defender coverage state (EDR coverage), precomputed so the coverage widgets are fast. The states marked gap are the genuine blind spots that the gap-list widgets and the coverage=gap / defender=gap filters select:

Out of scope

Never scanned, Scan is stale, Never reported and Sensor silent are surfaced but not counted as gaps. Not a scanning target / Not an endpoint target (printers, switches, appliances) and Out of scope (retired kit) are excluded from the coverage denominator so the percentage is honest.

The two coverage percentages are not the same measure. Tenable "covered" counts any Tenable record; Defender additionally excludes device classes no agent runs on. Compare each to itself over time, not to each other.

Reachability

The threat plugin also gives each asset an internet-facing verdict with its basis — Reachable (observed evidence), Ruled out (scanned, nothing listening) or Never looked (no port scan yet). This is what the attack-path widget's edge lane depends on; the full logic is on the Routes, delivery & reachability page. A verdict a person sets by hand is preserved across rule passes. The verdict is not shown on the Assets list or the asset page: it appears on the attack-path widget's reachability strip, and as the Reachable via column on the findings list when filtered to route=edge.

Filters & drill-downs

The list's controls are Search (hostname, IP, owner, serial), Type, Team, Site (including No site recorded), Scan coverage (any gap, or a specific Tenable state), Endpoint (no Defender sensor, or a specific state), Known by (a source, only:<source> for single-source machines to doubt, or not:<source>), Lifecycle (reporting scope, everything with an owner expectation, everything including retired, outside the reporting scope, out of service only, or one status) and Missing a user. Dashboard links can also carry primary_source, operating_system, patch_group and eol, shown as removable chips.

Columns: Host, Scan coverage (Tenable), EDR coverage (Defender), Known by, Type, Operating system, Owner (masked until revealed), Team, Open exposure. Sort by host, type, owner or exposure; more rows load as you scroll. Under 640px the list restacks as cards.

When the list is filtered by Known by (a source, or only that source), every row still shows all of its sources, but the one you filtered on is moved to the front of the Known by cell and highlighted with the accent colour and a dot, so you can scan down the column and confirm each row is what the filter says. Its tooltip notes that it is the filtered source. A not known by filter highlights nothing, because no row carries that source. Clicking any source chip filters the list to that source.

One asset

?asset=<id> opens a machine's page: Ownership (owner, email, department, team, location, and what resolved it), Identity and lifecycle (FQDN, serial, hardware and support end, the systems that know it with their scan and sensor dates, criticality, business service, environment, patch group, last seen), Cloud (account, region, instance, network) where relevant, Open exposure by severity with tags, and its Open findings.

Decommissioning

With triage permission, tick assets and choose Mark decommissioned, Return to service, or Another status…; the same buttons sit on a single asset's page. Decommissioning takes the asset out of scan coverage and archives its open findings, so the totals stop counting risk you no longer carry. Nothing is deleted, and returning the asset to service puts every finding back as it was. You come back to the same filtered list.

The most valuable asset view is the gap list. A production server the CMDB vouches for but Tenable has never scanned is precisely the machine your dashboard can't see findings on — so the coverage-gap widgets, which land here, are often more important than the findings you already have.
