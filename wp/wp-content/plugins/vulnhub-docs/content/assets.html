The Assets screen is the register of every machine VulnHub knows about — merged from all your systems into one row each — with its owner, what has and hasn't scanned it, whether the internet can reach it, and the findings it carries. It is where coverage-gap drill-downs land.

What one asset row represents

A single machine may be discovered by Tenable, managed by Intune, recorded in the CMDB and seen by Defender. VulnHub merges them by matching on the strongest identifier available (Tenable UUID → Intune id → Azure AD id → serial → hostname) and remembers every contributing system. So one row can carry facts from four sources, and the "Discovered by" / "Known from" column tells you which.

Key fields

Hostname / IP / OSIdentity and platform. The OS release is what the End-of-life widgets match against (by build number, not label).
TypeWorkstation, server, mobile, network, cloud, appliance or unknown — set by the classification rules or a connector.
Criticality & environmentHow much the machine matters, and where it sits (production/test/…). Criticality multiplies the risk score of its findings.
Owner / team / locationWho to chase. Resolved by ownership mapping — from the Intune primary user (high confidence), or the CMDB custodian (medium), or a fallback (low).
Lifecycle statusIn service, quarantine, maintenance, spare, stock, planned, retired, missing or unknown — drives whether the asset is "reportable" and whether its findings are counted or archived.
Open critical / highPre-computed roll-ups of the asset's live findings by severity, and its overall risk score.

Coverage — the blind-spot view

Every asset carries a Tenable coverage state and a Defender coverage state, precomputed so the coverage widgets are fast:

CoveredTenable/Defender has a current record of it.
Unscanned / silentKnown to the tool but not scanned in the freshness window (Tenable), or onboarded but the sensor is quiet (Defender). Surfaced, but not counted as a gap.
GapNot in the tool at all, or no recent contact from anything — a genuine blind spot. This is what the "gap list" widgets and the coverage=gap / defender=gap filters select.
Out of scope / other deviceRetired kit, or device classes nobody scans (printers, switches) — excluded from the coverage denominator so the percentage is honest.

The two coverage percentages are not the same measure. Tenable "covered" counts any Tenable record; Defender additionally excludes device classes no agent runs on. Compare each to itself over time, not to each other.

Reachability

Assets also carry an internet-facing verdict with its basis — Reachable (observed evidence), Ruled out (scanned, nothing listening) or Never looked (no port scan yet). This is what the attack-path widget's edge lane depends on; the full logic is on the Routes, delivery & reachability page. A verdict a person sets by hand is preserved across rule passes.

Filters & drill-downs

The asset list can be filtered by coverage state (coverage=gap, defender=gap), lifecycle scope (life=reportable and friends), the system that knows it (known=cmdb, or known=only:intune for single-source machines to doubt), device type, team, location (location_id=none = unplaced), ownership gap (needs_user=1), and end-of-life release. A single asset opens to its full detail — identity, sources, coverage, reachability, and its findings.

The most valuable asset view is the gap list. A production server the CMDB vouches for but Tenable has never scanned is precisely the machine your dashboard can't see findings on — so the coverage-gap widgets, which land here, are often more important than the findings you already have.
