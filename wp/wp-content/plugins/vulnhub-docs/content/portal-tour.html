Everything in VulnHub lives in one portal, with a dark "attack surface" design by default. Navigation is a 64px icon rail down the left edge; hover it (or tab into it) to reveal the labels. At the foot of the rail are the light/dark toggle (your choice is remembered in this browser), the Administration link for administrators, and your account menu with Security & MFA and Sign out. The page itself sits in a centred column beside the rail, so on a wide or zoomed-out screen the spare space is shared evenly on both sides. On a phone the rail becomes a bar across the top and the page uses the full width. This page walks through each destination.

The primary navigation

In rail order:

DashboardThe widget board — the at-a-glance picture of exposure, coverage, ownership and remediation. Fully covered in The dashboard, widget by widget.
VulnerabilitiesThe findings list, with Findings, By product and Vulnerability on assets tabs over the same filters. This is where dashboard drill-downs land. See The Vulnerabilities list.
AlertsAdvisory and zero-day alerts from the configured feeds, with the assets each one touches (present when the advisory-watch plugin is active). The icon carries a count of new alerts that match your estate.
AssetsThe asset register: every machine, its owner, scan and endpoint coverage, the systems that know it, and its open exposure. See Assets & owners.
EOL planThe end-of-support remediation programme: which machines past end of life have a funded project and a date, which do not, and what is due in each quarter. Covered start to finish in Lifecycle & exceptions.
TicketsRemediation tickets raised in Jira, their status, and the verification result when they close. See Tickets & exceptions.
ExceptionsThe risk-acceptance register: formally accepted risks, with reasons, approvers and expiry dates.
VendorsEvery hardware maker and software owner in the estate, with exposure, clickable counts (assets, products, findings, critical, uncovered, archived), advisory and risk-rating links; filter by Hardware / Software / Uncovered. See Products & vendors.
DocsThis handbook and developer wiki.

Pages built in Elementor on VulnHub data (for example Security overview and Estate and ownership) appear in the rail after Docs. Products (exposure grouped by software product) is reached from the "View all" control on the dashboard's Exposure by product widget rather than from the rail, and the full departments list from the Vulnerabilities by department widget.

The Administration area

Administrators reach the admin area from the Administration link at the foot of the rail (it only appears if you hold the manage capability). A Filter sections… box at the top of its side nav finds a section by name. Sections are grouped into Data, Integrations and Platform; the exact list depends on which plugins are active.

Data

ImportsUpload large CMDB and Tenable CSV exports (resumable, up to very large files).
RulesThe asset-classification rules — categorise and prioritise assets and decide who owns what.
Advisory feedsThe sources the Alerts screen reads; generic RSS/JSON adapters take a URL.
DepartmentsUpload an Entra user export to fill in each existing person's department, then filter and chart by it. See Departments.
Threat contextWhere exploit intelligence comes from, the EPSS threshold, and the reachability (exposure) rule.
EOS remediation planUpload the retirement programme's reconciled sheet — the treatment plan and timeframe per server. It previews before it commits, matches on hostname, and lists any hostname it could not find in the inventory.
Teams & SLAsRemediation owners, Jira routing and per-severity response targets.
Jira routingWhich service desk, request type and team a ticket lands on.

Integrations

IntegrationsConfigure, test and sync each connector. A red dot on this entry means a connector's last run failed.
AWS accountsEvery AWS account read for network exposure, and what each last managed to read.
Tenable, Intune, CMDB, Jira, Automation, Backupwp-admin screens mirrored into the portal (marked WP).

Platform

Admin overviewConnector health, data freshness, and what needs attention.
Authentication, End of lifeMirrored wp-admin screens (marked WP): sign-in, SSO and MFA policy; the end-of-life release table.
PeoplePortal accounts, their roles, and who still has access.
Your securityYour own multi-factor authentication: enrol an authenticator app, see and regenerate recovery codes. When the MFA policy requires it of your role, the portal sends you here until you have enrolled. This is where the account menu's Security & MFA link goes.
AI accessConnect an AI assistant (MCP) with your own permissions.
Sync activityEvery connector run, with its full log. Start here when data looks stale.
Audit trailWho changed what, and when.
AppearanceThe portal header/footer and the Elementor pages built on VulnHub data.
SettingsPlatform behaviour, ownership policy and retention.
DocumentationA link straight to this wiki.

The admin shell also shows a MOCK DATA badge while sample data is on, connector health in its top bar, and when each source last refreshed in its footer. See the Portal framework page for how sections are registered and gated.

Who can see what

Access is capability-based. In short: Viewers can read the portal; Analysts can also triage, raise tickets, request exceptions, run syncs and read the audit trail; Approvers can read, request and approve exceptions and read the audit trail; Administrators can do everything, including configure connectors. The Administration link needs the manage capability; a few admin sections (Sync activity, AI access, Documentation and some mirrored screens) only need view access but are then reached by URL. A "portal-only" user is one who can use the portal but is kept out of the WordPress admin. The capability table is on Filters & extension points.

Getting around fast. Almost every number on the dashboard is a link into the Vulnerabilities or Assets list with the right filters already applied — so the quickest way to "the list behind this figure" is to click the figure, not to rebuild the filter by hand.
